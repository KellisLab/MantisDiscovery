\mdsec{The question}
The Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality bounds entropy by Fisher information, and Gaussians make it sharp.
If a density nearly attains it, how close must the density be to a Gaussian, and can the constants be
independent of the dimension?
\mdgap
\mdsec{How we are approaching it}
\begin{itemize}
\item Study the transport first variation of the deficit: how the gap changes when the density is moved a little.
\item Find its smallest possible size at fixed Fisher information and second moment, and every case where the minimum is reached.
\item Integrate it along the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck flow, which carries any density to the Gaussian.
\end{itemize}
\mdgap
\mdsec{What we have so far}
\begin{progress}
We determine the smallest squared norm of the first variation at prescribed Fisher information and a second-moment cap, together with every case of equality.
\end{progress}
\begin{progress}
Integrated along the flow, it controls Wasserstein distance, Fisher information and the deficit with sharp constants, with no covariance or Poincar\'e assumption. Centered isotropic Gaussians give equality.
\end{progress}
\end{mdcol}
}

\mdsec{Open questions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The escaping parts of minimizing sequences need not be Gaussian. What can they look like?
\item The flow estimates extend to closed weighted manifolds with positive weighted Ricci curvature. How much further do they go?
\end{itemize}
