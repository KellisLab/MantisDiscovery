\mdsec{The question}
Two players move a token around a graph. A player may claim a repetition, budgets expire as play goes on,
and nothing resets on a directed cycle. A monitor watching the game must report its exact value at every
moment. How much of the history does the monitor have to remember?
\mdgap
\mdsec{How we are approaching it}
\begin{itemize}
\item Reduce a history to a draw boundary in the current strongly connected component, plus one bit for a current claim.
\item Upper bound: forget counts that cannot reach the repetition threshold $k$ before the budget runs out.
\item Lower bound: find common continuations that tell apart histories of the same length.
\end{itemize}
\mdgap
\mdsec{What we have so far}
\begin{progress}
For arenas with $N \ge 8$ vertices, no memory is needed when $H \le 2k - 2$. Otherwise exact monitoring needs $\Theta\bigl(\min\{N\log k,\ \log\binom{N+S}{S}\}\bigr)$ bits; the cases $k = 2$, $H \in \{3, 4\}$ need exactly one bit.
\end{progress}
\begin{progress}
Randomization with error below $1/2$ does not beat the dense $\Theta(N\log k)$ bound.
\end{progress}
\end{mdcol}
}

\end{progress}
\mdgap
\mdsec{Open questions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The memory bounds are proved for arenas with at least eight vertices. What happens on smaller ones?
\item The history results reach infinite arenas with a well-founded potential for resets. Which other infinite arenas do they cover?
\end{itemize}
